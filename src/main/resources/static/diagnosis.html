Diagnosis Journey

1. Possible Symptoms

Parents or caregivers notice delays in physical milestones or muscle weakness.

↓

2. Medical Evaluation

A pediatrician or family doctor performs an initial physical exam and review of history.

↓

3. Genetic Testing

The gold standard for diagnosis. A simple blood test looks for the SMN1 gene mutation or deletion.

↓

4. Diagnosis

Genetic testing confirms the presence of SMA and determines the number of SMN2 backup copies.

↓

5. Specialist Care

A multidisciplinary team (Neurologists, Pulmonologists, PTs) begins management.

Why Early Diagnosis Matters

SMA is a progressive disease. The earlier it is diagnosed, the sooner treatment can begin, which is crucial for preserving motor function and improving long-term outcomes.
