About Spinal Muscular Atrophy

What is SMA?

Spinal Muscular Atrophy (SMA) is a genetic disease that affects motor neurons—nerve cells in the spinal cord that control muscle movement. When motor neurons die, muscles weaken and waste away (atrophy).

SMA affects an individual's ability to walk, eat, and breathe. It is a leading genetic cause of death for infants.

Causes

SMA is caused by a mutation or deletion in the **SMN1 (Survival Motor Neuron 1)** gene. This gene is responsible for producing the SMN protein, which is essential for the survival and health of motor neurons.

Most cases of SMA are inherited in an autosomal recessive pattern, meaning both parents must carry the mutated gene and pass it on to their child.

Types of SMA

Type 1 (Werdnig-Hoffmann)

Onset: Before 6 months. Infants usually cannot sit without support.

Type 2

Onset: 6-18 months. Children can sit but typically cannot stand or walk independently.

Type 3 (Kugelberg-Welander)

Onset: After 18 months. Children can stand and walk, but may lose this ability later.

Type 4

Onset: Adulthood (usually after age 20-30). Symptoms are typically mild.
